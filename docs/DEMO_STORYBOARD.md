## The "why" — the problem we are solving

UK public bodies are under acute and rising Freedom of Information (FOI) pressure:

- **Record volumes, falling timeliness.** Central government received **70,475 FOI requests in 2023 — up 34%** on 2022 and the most since monitoring began in 2005. Only **81% were answered in time** (down from 86%), and just **34% were granted in full** — the lowest on record. (Cabinet Office, *FOI Statistics: Annual 2023*; Institute for Government.)
- **Regulator enforcement is escalating.** The Foreign Office has missed the Information Commissioner's 90% timeliness threshold every quarter since 2020; the **Ministry of Defence received a second enforcement notice (May 2026)** and the **Department for Business and Trade one in April 2026**. In **April 2026 the Commissioner issued practice recommendations to ~10 councils** simply for failing to *publish* their compliance statistics. (ICO FOI regulatory action.)
- **AI is now driving demand.** The Commissioner's 2025/26 guidance notes AI-drafted requests are longer, broader and harder to interpret (sometimes citing non-existent case law), and fuel round-robin/campaign requests — a new structural pressure. (ICO; mySociety.)
- **Local government is squeezed.** Councils juggle FOI, Environmental Information requests, Subject Access Requests and complaints with reduced staffing; hotspots are special educational needs (SEND), adult social care, housing and planning — the same areas that dominate Ombudsman complaints.
- **Internal-review backlogs.** Only **50% of internal reviews were completed within 20 working days** in 2023.

**The statutory clock never stops:** Freedom of Information **20 working days**; Environmental Information **20, up to 40** for complex cases; Subject Access Requests **1 calendar month**. The regulator monitors authorities that fall below **~90% in time**.

**The gap:** existing case-management tools are workflow engines built for a pre-AI world. They store and route requests but offer little intelligent triage, no duplicate/already-published detection, weak analytics, and sit *apart* from the data being requested. That is the gap FOI Sentinel fills — on Snowflake, where the data already lives.

## Scene-by-scene

1. **Command Centre (headlines).** Open the app. Lead with the integrated headline band: **Open requests** with at-risk and overdue nested inside, **Closed** as the converse, and the in-time percentage on the gauge against the **90% target**. Reference the falling-timeliness national picture. Click **"View at risk"** to drill straight into a filtered board — headlines are actionable.
2. **Statutory deadlines callout.** Pause on the deadlines strip (20 / 20–40 / 1 month). This is the relentless pressure the tool is built around.
3. **Intelligence (powered by Cortex).** Scroll to the word cloud, the emerging-themes trend and the repeat-requester table. Make the point: a storage-only case system cannot do this — Snowflake analyses the whole request corpus *in place* (themes, trends, campaign signals for section 14).
4. **Cases - the board.** The lifecycle board groups the 17 stages into 6 phases on a drag-and-drop Kanban (a custom React component using @hello-pangea/dnd, bundled into the image). Each case is a tile showing its reference and an AI-generated title (e.g. "SEND transport spend"), with a RAG dot for the deadline. **Drag** a card to another phase to advance it (the move is persisted and audit-logged); **click** a card to load the full case below: AI summary, full text on demand, the statutory clock, cost (the four prescribed activities), public-interest test, redaction (AI-suggested, human-verified), similar past responses (Camden precedent), and the compliant **draft response** generated by Cortex - all in one place.
5. **Email Intake (Testing).** Compose an FOI email, or **generate one with AI** at a chosen tone (hostile to appreciative, optionally seeded from a real Camden topic), then "send" it and watch it get classified live; or drop a `.eml`. Note that cases created here are flagged **synthetic** and excluded from the corpus analytics. Explain the production pipeline: shared mailbox -> Microsoft Graph/Power Automate -> Snowflake stage -> Snowpipe -> task -> triage.
6. **Triage Learning.** The differentiator: confidence-routed triage (auto-accept high-confidence, human-review the rest), human corrections becoming labelled training data, and a **genuine Cortex fine-tune** with base-vs-fine-tuned accuracy on a held-out set. Tie back to the HM Land Registry finding: *curated, class-balanced labels beat volume and prompting.*
7. **Knowledge & Guidance.** Search or pick a theme; open the AI advisor widget for tailored, guidance-grounded help.
8. **Escalations + Reviews & ICO + About.** Use the Escalations page to raise a synthetic internal review or ICO complaint, then in Reviews & ICO action it: track the 20-working-day review deadline (the ~50%-in-time pain point), generate the **review outcome letter** with Cortex, and produce the **ICO submission pack** (the case audit trail) that an authority sends to the Commissioner. Close on the architecture/integration diagram and the residency-aware production pattern (fine-tune in Frankfurt -> replicate to London -> infer in-region).
